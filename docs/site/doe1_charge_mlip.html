Charge-Aware MLIPs for Surface Iodine Chemistry

Can a charge-conditioned, multifidelity MLIP help explain why illuminated FAPbI3 surfaces retain or lose iodine, in a way conventional DFT and existing MLIPs cannot do efficiently? Short answer: for one piece of the problem, yes. It does not answer the whole question.

Recommendation

CONDITIONAL GO, after the proposal

Mention it in DOE_1 as a gated methods capability, not as a central claim. Before submission, run one zero-HPC feasibility step: inspect the published code and data, which needs your OK for a 102 MB download. Authorise the full pilot only if a small electronic sanity check passes. That check asks whether hybrid+SOC DFT localises the hole on a surface I–I species in our slab model, and whether the charge states give genuinely different relaxed structures.

The method directly addresses the thermal sampling of charged surface iodine configurations. That supports the 25–55 °C prediction and the identity of the iodine species. It does not model photoexcitation, carrier capture, recombination, or the atmosphere. The existing DFT/NAMD workflow still has to cover those.

The closer precedent is Mosquera-Lois and Walsh, arXiv:2508.14513 (2025), on V_Cl in CsPbCl3:

  • Method: multi-head MACE with PBE and HSE+SOC heads (α = 0.375, tuned to the 300 K gap), but with separate models per charge state rather than a charge embedding. MD in 160–640-atom cells. A Kumagai–Oba correction is applied after the fact.
  • Finding: the optical level fluctuates by more than 1 eV at 300 K, while the thermodynamic (+1/0) level stays within about 0.1 eV of its static value.
  • Relevance: this is the right template for halide perovskites, because it uses SOC and finite temperature. It has no iodide, no surface and no organic cation.

The Sb2Se3 errors (RMSE about 0.5 meV/atom and 20 meV/Å) and the claimed savings should not be carried over to FAPbI3 surfaces. SOC, organic cations, dispersion and slab electrostatics are all absent from that benchmark.

4. Minimal pilot (prepared, not launched)

Motif and model

  • Motif: surface iodine interstitial on PbI2-rich FAPbI3(100). This is chosen because I_i ↔ I–I dimer ↔ I2 is the path that separates iodine retention from release.
  • Configurations (to be verified):
    • a bridging I_i;
    • a surface I–I dimer, the I2⁻ candidate;
    • an I3⁻-like trimer;
    • an I2 molecule adsorbed or desorbed as the release endpoint.
  • Charge states: q = −1, 0, +1, kept only where the reference geometries and spin densities actually differ.
  • Slab: reduced symmetric slab, 2×2 in-plane (about 12.7 Å), 5 layers (PbI2/FAI/PbI2/FAI/PbI2), roughly 150–180 atoms. Starting motifs are transplanted from the archived r2SCAN 567-atom structures.
  • Control: a 2×2×2 bulk cell (96 atoms) to calibrate finite-size corrections.

Electronic settings

  • Low fidelity: r2SCAN+D3(BJ), our tool profile, with ISPIN=2, ISIF=2, fixed cell, LDIPOL, 520 eV, Γ plus a k-convergence test.
  • High fidelity: HSE(α)+SOC single points, with α tuned to the FAPbI3 gap and D3(BJ) kept identical across heads.
  • Spin check: an SOC and spin-polarisation check on every odd-electron configuration.
  • Electrostatics: charged slabs use a fixed vacuum. Test 25, 35 and 45 Å, and use a slab-specific correction (Freysoldt–Neugebauer type), not a bulk eFNV correction. Defects go on both faces symmetrically, or a dipole correction is applied.
  • Localisation check: before any label is trusted, check hole localisation with spin density and IPR, and check that the SCF lands on the same electronic state when started from different guesses.

5. Validation and acceptance criteria

  • Splits are made by configuration family and trajectory, never by frame. One whole motif (for example the I3⁻ trimer) and one whole NEB path are held out.
  • Structure recovery: after relaxation by the model, the hybrid+SOC ground-state geometry is recovered per q with local RMSD < 0.1 Å, and the same motif is reached from ShakeNBreak starts.
  • Relative stability: the ordering of competing iodine configurations per q is correct, with |ΔΔE| < 0.05 eV per pair.
  • Charge-transition levels are within 0.1 eV of HSE+SOC, after the same correction is applied to both.
  • Barriers for I_i hopping and dimer dissociation or desorption are within 0.1 eV of the hybrid NEB on held-out paths.
  • Forces are reported per q and per family, together with failure tails.
  • Value of hybrid data: M2 must beat M1 on stability ordering or levels by more than the M1 error bar. Otherwise the HSE cost is not justified.
  • Physics checks: if the q-states relax to the same structures, charge conditioning is unnecessary. If HSE+SOC does not localise the hole, problem B cannot be addressed this way.

7. Paragraph for the proposal

Whether illuminated surfaces retain or lose iodine depends on how photogenerated holes trapped at surface iodine change the free-energy landscape linking interstitial iodide, I–I bonded species, and volatile I2. Hybrid-functional calculations with spin–orbit coupling are needed to localise these holes correctly, but they are too costly for the finite-temperature sampling required to resolve trends across 25–55 °C. We will therefore test, behind an explicit decision gate, a charge-conditioned multifidelity interatomic potential (after Wang, Mosquera-Lois and Walsh, PRX Energy 2026). It will be trained mainly on r2SCAN data and calibrated with a targeted ~10% set of hybrid+SOC reference calculations, and used to compute charge-resolved stabilities and barriers for these iodine transformations at operating temperatures. We will adopt it only if it reproduces held-out hybrid-level structures, configuration orderings, and barriers. Photoexcitation, carrier capture, and recombination remain within our nonadiabatic dynamics framework. The potential supplies the charge-state-specific ground-state energetics that those kinetics require.
